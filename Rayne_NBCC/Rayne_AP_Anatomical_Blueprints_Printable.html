1. Plasma Membrane & Cytoplasm

  • Plasma Membrane: Semipermeable lipid bilayer enclosing the entire cell. Protects cell contents while allowing selective passage of nutrients and waste.
  • Cytoplasm: Internal gel-like fluid medium (cytosol) that houses all cell organelles, ions, and enzymes.

2. Nucleus ("Control Center")

  • Function: Directs all cellular activities, growth, and division.
  • Genetic Content: Houses DNA organized into 23 pairs (46 total) of chromosomes.

3. Mitochondria ("Powerhouse")

  • Function: Extracts chemical energy from nutrients (glucose/oxygen).
  • Output: Converts food energy into ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate), the universal cellular fuel.

4. Ribosomes ("Protein Factories")

  • Function: Translate genetic code into functional structural and enzymatic proteins.
  • Location: Float free in cytoplasm or attach to Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER).

Skin Layers

  • Epidermis: Nonvascular outer shield. 5 layers from deep to surface: Basale → Spinosum → Granulosum → Lucidum → Corneum.
  • Dermis: Mechanical strength layer. Papillary (fingerprints) & Reticular (collagen/elastin).
  • Hypodermis: Fat layer for insulation, cushioning, & anchoring.

Glands of the Skin

  • Eccrine Sweat Glands: Abundant all over body; thin watery sweat for cooling & waste excretion.
  • Apocrine Sweat Glands: Axilla & groin; thicker fluid producing body odor when broken down by skin bacteria.
  • Sebaceous Glands: Oil glands secreting sebum to prevent skin cracking.

Hair & Nails

  • Hair: Shaft (visible), Root (in follicle), Papilla/Bulb (growth site). Arrector Pili muscle contracts for goosebumps.
  • Nails: Hardened keratin plate. Body, Root (under cuticle), Lunula (white crescent).
  • Cyanosis: Bluish nail beds from poor blood oxygenation.

5 Functions of the Skeleton

  • 1. Support & Shape: Structural framework for body.
  • 2. Protection: Encloses brain, spinal cord, heart, lungs.
  • 3. Movement: Tendons attach muscle to bone for levers.
  • 4. Storage: Primary reservoir for calcium.
  • 5. Hematopoiesis: Blood cell creation in red bone marrow.

5 Types of Bones with Examples

  • Long Bones: Femur, humerus, tibia (longer than wide).
  • Short Bones: Carpals (wrist), tarsals (ankle).
  • Flat Bones: Scapula, sternum, ribs, cranium.
  • Irregular Bones: Vertebrae, sacrum, mandible.
  • Sesamoid Bones: Patella (kneecap embedded in tendon).

Long Bone Anatomy

  • Diaphysis: Shaft with medullary cavity (yellow marrow).
  • Epiphysis: Ends (site of growth at epiphyseal plate).
  • Periosteum: Outer fibrous membrane.
  • Endosteum: Membrane lining cavity.

3 Cartilage Types

  • Hyaline (Articular): Smooth glass-like cushion at joints & rib connections.
  • Elastic: Springy cartilage in ear & epiglottis.
  • Fibrocartilage: Tough shock absorber in intervertebral discs & pubic symphysis.

Joint Classifications

  • Synarthroses: Immovable joints (e.g. skull sutures).
  • Amphiarthroses: Slightly movable (e.g. pubic symphysis).
  • Diarthroses: Freely movable synovial joints (e.g. shoulder, hip, knee).
