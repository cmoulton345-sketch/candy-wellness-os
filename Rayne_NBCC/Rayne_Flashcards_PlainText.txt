COMMUNICATIONS 1 (NCSI1182B) - FLASHCARDS (MODULES 1 TO 3)
Target Student: Rayne Moulton (Practical Nursing / NBCC)


MODULE 1: BASIC COMMUNICATION

FLASHCARD 1
FRONT: What is Communication?
BACK: A complex, ongoing two-way process of sharing ideas, thoughts, and feelings. It involves a sender encoding a message, transmitting through a channel, a receiver decoding the message, and providing feedback to complete the cycle.

FLASHCARD 2
FRONT: What is Nonverbal Communication & why is it crucial?
BACK: Physical gestures, facial expressions, body posture, eye contact, tone, pitch, and proxemics. It accounts for 80% to 93% of face-to-face communication impact. When nonverbal cues contradict verbal words, people trust the nonverbal cues!

FLASHCARD 3
FRONT: What are the 4 Zones of Proxemics (Space)?
BACK:
1. Intimate Space: 0 to 45 cm (0 to 18 inches) - Close emotional ties or physical care.
2. Personal Distance: 45 cm to 1.2 meters (18 inches to 3.5 feet) - Arm's reach; ideal for history taking.
3. Social Distance: 1.2 meters to 3.6 meters (3.5 to 12 feet) - Formal / business relationships.
4. Public Distance: 3.6 meters outward (12 feet+) - Out in public / non-personal settings.

FLASHCARD 4
FRONT: What is Metacommunication?
BACK: Broad term for all factors (verbal and nonverbal cues, posture, eye contact, expressions) that provide instructions on how to interpret the message being delivered.

FLASHCARD 5
FRONT: What is Congruent Communication vs Incongruent Communication?
BACK:
Congruent: Spoken words and nonverbal body language match perfectly (example: patient says "I am relaxed" and sits calmly).
Incongruent: Words and nonverbal body language conflict (example: patient says "No pain" while grimacing and guarding abdomen). Nurse must explore incongruence!

FLASHCARD 6
FRONT: Social Communication vs Professional Therapeutic Communication
BACK:
Social: Casual conversation with peers/family, unwritten rules, driven by mutual pleasure and self-interest.
Professional Therapeutic: Goal-directed, purposeful, evidence-based clinical interaction bound by legal and ethical standards to promote patient health and safety.


MODULE 2: EFFECTIVE COMMUNICATION (BRIDGES)

FLASHCARD 7
FRONT: What is Respect in Nursing Communication?
BACK: Valuing patient knowledge, opinions, and dignity. Always address patients by their preferred name. Avoid patronizing terms like "Honey", "Dear", or "Sweetie", and avoid asking "How are we feeling today?"

FLASHCARD 8
FRONT: What are Roach's 6 C's of Caring?
BACK:
1. Compassion (feeling with the patient)
2. Competence (knowledge and skill)
3. Confidence (fostering trust)
4. Conscience (ethical integrity)
5. Commitment (pledge to care)
6. Comportment (professional demeanor)

FLASHCARD 9
FRONT: What is Swanson's Caring Theory (1991)?
BACK:
Knowing: Understanding the meaning of an event in the patient's life.
Being: Emotionally present and sharing feelings.
Doing: Performing nursing interventions to preserve integrity.

FLASHCARD 10
FRONT: What is Mutuality in Nursing?
BACK: Collaborative relationship where nurse and patient share decision-making, agree on health goals, and work as equal partners. Respects patient autonomy even if choices differ from nurse's opinion.

FLASHCARD 11
FRONT: What is Empowerment vs Disempowerment?
BACK:
Empowerment: Assisting patients to take charge of their lives by providing tools, education, and confidence for self-management.
Disempowerment: Demonstrating a paternalistic "I know best" attitude that strips patient autonomy and choice.

FLASHCARD 12
FRONT: What is Empathy vs Sympathy?
BACK:
Empathy: Ability to put oneself in another's shoes, understanding their perspective nonjudgmentally while keeping professional objectivity.
Sympathy: Feeling pity or sorrow for someone, which can blur clinical judgment and boundaries.


MODULE 3: BARRIERS TO EFFECTIVE COMMUNICATION

FLASHCARD 13
FRONT: Why is False Reassurance a major communication barrier?
BACK: Saying "Don't worry, everything will be fine" offers ungrounded hope, invalidates genuine patient fears, destroys trust, and blocks the patient from expressing true feelings.

FLASHCARD 14
FRONT: Bias vs Stereotype - What is the difference?
BACK:
Bias: A personal preference, like, or dislike that interferes with impartiality.
Stereotype: A fixed, preconceived idea attributing specific characteristics to all members of a category or group.

FLASHCARD 15
FRONT: Why is Giving Advice / Opinions non-therapeutic?
BACK: Saying "If I were you..." takes away patient decision-making and fosters dependence. Therapeutic alternative: Reflecting (example: "What options are you considering?") to empower patient decision-making.

FLASHCARD 16
FRONT: Why is Arguing with a patient harmful?
BACK: Challenging or arguing patient perceptions implies they are lying or misinformed, destroys rapport, displays unprofessional behavior, and escalates conflict instead of resolving underlying anxiety.

FLASHCARD 17
FRONT: How does Anxiety affect patient communication?
BACK: High anxiety causes distracted thinking, hyper-focus on negative cues, overthinking sentences, and impaired listening/comprehension. Nurses must respond with calm, slow, unhurried, clear statements.

FLASHCARD 18
FRONT: What are 4 Key Nursing Strategies to Reduce Patient Anxiety?
BACK:
1. Active listening and validating feelings.
2. Honest, simple explanations of procedures and tests.
3. Calm, slow, firm, unhurried communication.
4. Teach relaxation techniques (deep breathing, guided imagery, touch when appropriate).
