MODULE 1: INTRODUCTION TO THE BODY & HOMEOSTASIS

FLASHCARD 1
FRONT: What are the 6 Levels of Body Organization (Simplest to Complex)?
BACK:
1. Chemical: Atoms and molecules.
2. Cellular: Cells (basic unit of life).
3. Tissue: Group of similar cells.
4. Organ: Group of tissues working together.
5. Organ System: Group of organs contributing to a function.
6. Organism: The complete living human being.

FLASHCARD 2
FRONT: What is Homeostasis?
BACK: The state of relative internal body balance and stability maintained by self-regulating feedback mechanisms despite external changes.

FLASHCARD 3
FRONT: Negative Feedback vs Positive Feedback Loops
BACK:
Negative Feedback: Opposes or negates a change to restore normal levels (example: regulating body temperature, blood glucose).
Positive Feedback: Enhances or amplifies a change occurring in the body (example: uterine contractions during childbirth, blood clotting cascade).

FLASHCARD 4
FRONT: What are the 2 Major Body Cavities & Subdivisions?
BACK:
Ventral (Front) Cavity: Thoracic (Mediastinum and Pleural) and Abdominopelvic (Abdominal and Pelvic).
Dorsal (Back) Cavity: Cranial (brain) and Spinal (spinal cord).

FLASHCARD 5
FRONT: What are the 4 Abdominopelvic Quadrants & Key Organs?
BACK:
RUQ: Liver, gallbladder, right kidney.
LUQ: Stomach, spleen, pancreas, left kidney.
RLQ: Appendix, cecum, right ovary/fallopian tube.
LLQ: Sigmoid colon, left ovary/fallopian tube.


CELL BIOLOGY: HUMAN CELL STRUCTURE & FUNCTION

FLASHCARD 6
FRONT: Plasma Membrane
BACK: Semipermeable lipid bilayer enclosing cell contents (cytoplasm). Delicate yet strong membrane that controls what enters and exits the cell.

FLASHCARD 7
FRONT: Nucleus
BACK: The cell's "control center" managing genetic material (DNA organized into chromosomes) that directs growth, division, and cell function.

FLASHCARD 8
FRONT: Ribosomes
BACK: The "protein factories" of the cell. Synthesize proteins; found floating free in cytoplasm or attached to Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER).

FLASHCARD 9
FRONT: Mitochondria
BACK: The "powerhouse" of the cell. Extracts energy from nutrients and converts it into ATP (adenosine triphosphate) chemical energy.


MODULE 3: THE INTEGUMENTARY SYSTEM

FLASHCARD 10
FRONT: Epidermis Vascularity & Cells
BACK: Outermost nonvascular layer (no blood vessels). 95% Keratinocytes (waterproof keratin protein) and 5% Melanocytes (melanin skin color pigment).

FLASHCARD 11
FRONT: Dermis & Dermal Papillae
BACK: Middle layer 20x thicker than epidermis packed with collagen and elastin. Dermal papillae in papillary layer form unique fingerprints and footprints.

FLASHCARD 12
FRONT: Hypodermis (Subcutaneous Tissue)
BACK: Foundation layer under skin composed mostly of adipose (fat) tissue for insulation, cushioning, energy storage, and attaching skin to underlying structures.

FLASHCARD 13
FRONT: Eccrine vs Apocrine Sweat Glands
BACK:
Eccrine: All over body; thin watery sweat for cooling and waste excretion.
Apocrine: Axilla and groin; thicker fluid producing body odor when broken down by skin bacteria.

FLASHCARD 14
FRONT: Cutaneous Mechanoreceptors (Sensory Receptors)
BACK:
Meissner's: Light touch / texture.
Pacinian: Deep pressure / vibration.
Krause: Cold sensation.
Ruffini: Skin stretch.
Free Nerve Endings: Pain and itch.


MODULE 4: THE SKELETAL SYSTEM & JOINTS

FLASHCARD 15
FRONT: What are the 5 Functions of the Skeletal System?
BACK:
1. Support and shape
2. Protection of vital organs
3. Movement (tendon levers)
4. Mineral storage (calcium)
5. Hematopoiesis (blood cell production in red bone marrow)

FLASHCARD 16
FRONT: What are the 5 Types of Bones with Examples?
BACK:
1. Long Bones: Femur, humerus, tibia.
2. Short Bones: Carpals, tarsals.
3. Flat Bones: Scapula, sternum, ribs, cranium.
4. Irregular Bones: Vertebrae, sacrum, mandible.
5. Sesamoid Bones: Patella (embedded in tendon).

FLASHCARD 17
FRONT: Structure of a Long Bone
BACK:
Diaphysis: Shaft containing medullary cavity (yellow marrow).
Epiphysis: Bone ends containing red marrow; site of growth at epiphyseal plate.
Periosteum: Strong fibrous membrane covering bone exterior.
Articular Cartilage: Hyaline cartilage cushioning bone ends.

FLASHCARD 18
FRONT: Axial vs Appendicular Skeleton
BACK:
Axial (80 bones): Skull, hyoid, vertebral column, ribs, sternum.
Appendicular (126 bones): Pectoral girdle, upper limbs, pelvic girdle, lower limbs.

FLASHCARD 19
FRONT: 3 Types of Cartilage
BACK:
Hyaline (Articular): Smooth glass-like; covers bone ends at joints and connects ribs to sternum.
Elastic: Springy; found in ear and epiglottis.
Fibrocartilage: Tough and fibrous; found in intervertebral discs and pubic symphysis.

FLASHCARD 20
FRONT: 3 Functional Joint Classifications
BACK:
Synarthroses: Immovable joints (example: skull sutures).
Amphiarthroses: Slightly movable joints (example: pubic symphysis, intervertebral joints).
Diarthroses: Freely movable synovial joints (example: shoulder, hip, knee, elbow).
