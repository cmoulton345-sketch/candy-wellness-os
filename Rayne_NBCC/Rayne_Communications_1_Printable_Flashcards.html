MODULE 1 CARD #1
Q: What is Communication?
A: A complex, ongoing two-way process of sharing ideas, thoughts, and feelings. It involves a sender encoding a message, transmitting through a channel, a receiver decoding the message, and providing feedback to complete the cycle.

MODULE 1 CARD #2
Q: What is Nonverbal Communication & why is it crucial?
A: Physical gestures, facial expressions, body posture, eye contact, tone, pitch, and proxemics. It accounts for 80–93% of face-to-face impact. When words and body language conflict, receivers trust body language!

MODULE 1 CARD #3
Q: What are the 4 Zones of Proxemics (Space)?
1. Intimate Space: 0–45 cm (0–18 in)
2. Personal Distance: 45 cm–1.2 m (18 in–3.5 ft)
3. Social Distance: 1.2 m–3.6 m (3.5–12 ft)
4. Public Distance: 3.6 m+ (12 ft+)

Q: What is Metacommunication?
A: Broad term for all factors (verbal/nonverbal cues, posture, eye contact, expressions) that provide instructions on how to interpret the message being delivered.

MODULE 1 CARD #5
Q: Congruent vs Incongruent Communication
Congruent: Spoken words and nonverbal body language match.
Incongruent: Words and body language conflict (e.g., saying "No pain" while grimacing). Nurse must explore incongruence!

MODULE 1 CARD #6
Q: Social vs Professional Therapeutic Communication
Social: Casual conversation, unwritten rules, driven by mutual pleasure.
Therapeutic: Goal-directed, purposeful, evidence-based clinical interaction bound by legal/ethical standards.

MODULE 2 CARD #7
Q: What is Respect in Nursing Communication?
A: Valuing patient knowledge, opinions, and dignity. Always address patients by preferred name. Avoid patronizing terms like "Honey", "Dear", or "Sweetie", and avoid "How are we feeling today?"

MODULE 2 CARD #8
Q: What are Roach's 6 C's of Caring?
1. Compassion (feeling with patient)
2. Competence (knowledge & skill)
3. Confidence (fostering trust)
4. Conscience (ethical integrity)
5. Commitment (pledge to care)
6. Comportment (professional demeanor)

MODULE 2 CARD #9
Q: What is Swanson's Caring Theory (1991)?
Knowing: Understanding the meaning of an event in the patient's life.
Being: Emotionally present and sharing feelings.
Doing: Performing nursing interventions to preserve integrity.

Q: What is Mutuality in Nursing?
A: Collaborative relationship where nurse and patient share decision-making, agree on health goals, and work as equal partners. Respects patient autonomy even if choices differ from nurse's opinion.

Q: What is Empowerment vs Disempowerment?
Empowerment: Assisting patients to take charge of their health with tools & education.
Disempowerment: Paternalistic "I know best" attitude stripping patient control.

Q: What is Empathy vs Sympathy?
Empathy: Understanding another's feelings nonjudgmentally while maintaining professional objectivity.
Sympathy: Feeling pity/sorrow, which can blur clinical judgment.

MODULE 3 CARD #13
Q: Why is False Reassurance harmful?
A: Saying "Don't worry, everything will be fine" offers ungrounded hope, invalidates genuine patient fears, destroys trust, and blocks the patient from expressing true feelings.

MODULE 3 CARD #14
Q: Bias vs Stereotype — What is the difference?
Bias: Personal preference, like, or dislike interfering with impartiality.
Stereotype: Fixed, preconceived idea attributing specific traits to ALL members of a group.

MODULE 3 CARD #15
Q: Why is Giving Advice non-therapeutic?
A: Stating "If I were you..." takes away patient decision-making and fosters dependence.
Therapeutic alternative: Reflecting (e.g., "What options are you considering?").

MODULE 3 CARD #16
Q: Why is Arguing with a patient harmful?
A: Challenging or arguing patient perceptions implies they are lying or misinformed, destroys rapport, displays unprofessional behavior, and escalates conflict.

MODULE 3 CARD #17
Q: How does Anxiety affect patient communication?
A: High anxiety causes distracted thinking, hyper-focus on negative cues, overthinking sentences, and impaired listening. Nurses must respond with calm, slow, unhurried, clear statements.

Q: 4 Strategies to Reduce Patient Anxiety
1. Active listening & validating feelings.
2. Honest, simple explanations of procedures.
3. Calm, slow, firm, unhurried communication.
4. Teach deep breathing & relaxation exercises.
