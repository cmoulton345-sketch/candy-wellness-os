Vocabulary terms for Module 8
The Endocrine system
Term | Definition
Endocrine System | A body system that communicates and controls body functions through hormones carried in the bloodstream.
Hormone | A chemical messenger that travels through the blood to regulate the activities of target cells and organs.
Target Cell | A cell that contains specific receptors for a hormone.
Endocrine Gland | A ductless gland that secretes hormones directly into the bloodstream.
Steroid Hormone | A lipid-soluble hormone that binds to receptors inside the cell.
Nonsteroid Hormone | A water-soluble hormone that binds to receptors on the outside of a cell.
Negative Feedback | A homeostatic mechanism that reverses a change and maintains balance within the body.
Positive Feedback | A mechanism that amplifies a change rather than reversing it.
Pituitary Gland | A pea-sized gland at the base of the brain often called the "master gland" because it controls many other endocrine glands.
Anterior Pituitary | The front lobe of the pituitary gland that secretes several major hormones.
Posterior Pituitary | The back lobe of the pituitary gland that releases ADH and oxytocin.
Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH) | A hormone that stimulates the thyroid gland to produce thyroid hormones.
Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH) | A hormone that stimulates the adrenal cortex to release hormones such as cortisol.
Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) | A hormone that stimulates egg development in females and sperm production in males.
Luteinizing Hormone (LH) | A hormone involved in ovulation and the production of reproductive hormones.
Growth Hormone (GH) | A hormone that stimulates growth and development of body tissues.
Prolactin (PRL) | A hormone that stimulates milk production in the mammary glands.
Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) | A hormone that promotes water retention by the kidneys.
Vasopressin | Another name for antidiuretic hormone (ADH).
Oxytocin | A hormone that stimulates uterine contractions and milk release during breastfeeding.
Hypothalamus | A brain structure that links the nervous and endocrine systems and regulates the pituitary gland.
Releasing Hormones (RHs) | Hormones produced by the hypothalamus that stimulate the anterior pituitary.
Inhibiting Hormones (IHs) | Hormones produced by the hypothalamus that suppress anterior pituitary hormone secretion.
Thyroid Gland | A butterfly-shaped gland in the neck that regulates metabolism, growth, and development.
Thyroxine (T4) | A thyroid hormone that helps regulate metabolism and energy use.
Triiodothyronine (T3) | A thyroid hormone that helps control metabolic activity.
Calcitonin (CT) | A hormone that lowers blood calcium levels by inhibiting bone breakdown.
Parathyroid Gland | Small glands located behind the thyroid that regulate calcium levels in the blood.
Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) | A hormone that raises blood calcium levels by stimulating bone breakdown.
Adrenal Glands | Triangular glands located on top of the kidneys that help regulate stress responses, metabolism, and blood pressure.
Adrenal Cortex | The outer portion of the adrenal gland that produces steroid hormones.
Adrenal Medulla | The inner portion of the adrenal gland that produces epinephrine and norepinephrine.
Aldosterone | A hormone that regulates fluid and electrolyte balance.
Cortisol | A hormone that helps regulate metabolism, blood sugar, inflammation, and stress responses.
Androgens | Hormones that promote male characteristics.
Estrogens | Hormones that promote female characteristics.
Epinephrine | A hormone involved in the body's fight-or-flight response; also known as adrenaline.
Norepinephrine | A hormone that helps regulate blood vessel constriction and the stress response.
Pineal Gland | A small gland in the brain that produces melatonin.
Melatonin | A hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles and circadian rhythms.
Circadian Rhythm | The body's internal clock that regulates daily sleep and activity patterns.
Thymus | A gland located behind the sternum that plays a role in immune system development.
Thymosin | A hormone that promotes the development of immune system cells.
T Cells | Immune cells that mature in the thymus and help fight infections.
Pancreas | An organ that functions in both the digestive and endocrine systems.
Islets of Langerhans | Clusters of endocrine cells within the pancreas that produce insulin and glucagon.
Insulin | A hormone that lowers blood glucose by promoting the movement of glucose into cells.
Glucagon | A hormone that raises blood glucose by stimulating glycogen breakdown in the liver.
Glycogenolysis | The breakdown of glycogen into glucose.
Blood Glucose | The amount of sugar present in the bloodstream.
Ovaries | Female reproductive glands that produce eggs and hormones.
Progesterone | A hormone that helps maintain conditions necessary for pregnancy.
Testes | Male reproductive glands that produce sperm and testosterone.
Testosterone | The primary male sex hormone responsible for male reproductive development and characteristics.
Gonads | The reproductive glands; ovaries in females and testes in males.
Metabolism | The sum of all chemical processes that occur within the body to maintain life.
Homeostasis | The maintenance of a stable internal environment within the body
