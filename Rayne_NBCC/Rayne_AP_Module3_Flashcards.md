## Part 1: Skin Layers & Cell Types

### Flashcard 1
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is the Epidermis and its vascularity?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** The outermost layer of skin composed of stratified squamous epithelium. It is **nonvascular** (has no blood vessels) and receives nutrients via diffusion from the dermis below.

---

### Flashcard 2
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are the 5 Layers of the Epidermis (Deep to Surface)?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  1. **Stratum Basale:** Deepest layer; active cell division (mitosis).
  2. **Stratum Spinosum:** Spiny layer; cellular strength.
  3. **Stratum Granulosum:** Granular layer; keratin production begins.
  4. **Stratum Lucidum:** Clear layer found ONLY in thick skin (palms, soles, lips).
  5. **Stratum Corneum:** Outermost layer of dead, flat, keratinized cells.

---

### Flashcard 3
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Keratinocytes vs Melanocytes
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Keratinocytes (95% of cells):** Produce tough fibrous protein *keratin* that waterproofs skin & prevents dehydration.
  * **Melanocytes (5% of cells):** Produce pigment *melanin* that absorbs UV radiation & determines skin color.

---

### Flashcard 4
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is the Dermis and its 2 Layers?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Middle layer 20x thicker than epidermis, packed with collagen & elastic fibers, blood vessels, and nerves.
  * **Papillary Layer:** Contains dermal papillae (fingerprints) & pain/touch receptors.
  * **Reticular Layer:** Deeper thick layer containing collagen/elastin, glands, hair follicles, & Pacinian pressure receptors.

---

### Flashcard 5
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is the Hypodermis (Subcutaneous Tissue)?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Foundation layer under skin composed mainly of **adipose (fat) tissue**. Functions: energy storage, thermal insulation, shock absorption/cushioning, and attaching skin to underlying muscle/bone.

## Part 2: Accessory Organs (Hair, Nails, Glands)

### Flashcard 6
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are the 3 Components of Hair & Arrector Pili Muscle?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Hair Shaft:** Visible part above skin.
  * **Hair Root:** Hidden portion inside follicle.
  * **Hair Papilla/Bulb:** Cap-shaped cluster of blood-nourished cells at base where growth begins.
  * **Arrector Pili Muscle:** Smooth muscle that contracts when cold/frightened to cause "goosebumps".

---

### Flashcard 7
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are the 3 Components of a Nail & Cyanosis?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Nail Body:** Visible plate made of hardened keratin.
  * **Nail Root:** Hidden under cuticle (eponychium).
  * **Lunula:** Crescent-shaped white area near root.
  * **Cyanosis:** Bluish discoloration of nail beds due to poor blood oxygen perfusion.

---

### Flashcard 8
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Eccrine vs Apocrine Sweat Glands
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Eccrine Glands:** Most abundant all over body; produce thin watery perspiration for temperature regulation & waste removal.
  * **Apocrine Glands:** Found in axilla, groin, nipples; produce thicker secretion that creates body odor when broken down by skin bacteria.

---

### Flashcard 9
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are Sebaceous Glands & Sebum?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Oil glands attached to hair follicles that secrete **sebum** ("nature's skin cream") to lubricate skin & prevent drying/cracking. Blocked ducts form whiteheads (pimples) or blackheads (oxidized sebum).

## Part 3: Cutaneous Mechanoreceptors (Sensation)

### Flashcard 10
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Meissner's Corpuscle
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Mechanoreceptor located in dermal papillae responsible for sensing **texture and light vibration / touch**.

---

### Flashcard 11
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Pacinian Corpuscle
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Deep mechanoreceptor located in reticular dermis/hypodermis responsible for sensing **deep pressure and fast vibration**.

---

### Flashcard 12
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Ruffini's End Organ
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Mechanoreceptor responsible for detecting **skin stretch**.

---

### Flashcard 13
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** End Bulb of Krause
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Cutaneous receptor specialized for detecting **cold temperatures**.

---

### Flashcard 14
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Merkel's Disc
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Sensory receptor responsible for detecting **sustained touch and pressure**.

---

### Flashcard 15
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Free Nerve Endings
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Unencapsulated nerve endings throughout skin that signal **pain and itching**.

---

## Part 4: 6 Core Functions of the Skin

### Flashcard 16
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does the skin provide Protection?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Acts as body's first line of defense against microbial invasion, UV radiation, chemical exposure, physical cuts, and dehydration.

---

### Flashcard 17
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does the skin regulate Temperature?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Heat Loss:** Sweating (evaporative cooling) & dermal vasodilation.
  * **Heat Retention:** Dermal vasoconstriction, shivering, & arrector pili muscle contraction (goosebumps).

---

### Flashcard 18
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does the skin function in Excretion?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Eliminates metabolic waste products (urea, uric acid, ammonia, salts, water) through sweat gland activity.

---

### Flashcard 19
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does the skin synthesize Vitamin D?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** UV light exposure converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin cells into Vitamin D precursors, which are subsequently activated by enzymes in the **liver and kidneys**.

---

### Flashcard 20
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does the skin function in Absorption?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Absorbs lipid-soluble substances via passive diffusion (e.g., transdermal medication patches like nitroglycerin, nicotine, hormone creams, as well as lipid toxins).
