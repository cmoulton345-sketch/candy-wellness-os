ATLAS: 1. Human Cell Ultrastructure & Organelles
Cell Biology (Module 2 • Chapter 3)
Nucleus The control center of the cell; houses DNA chromosomes and directs protein synthesis.
Nucleoli (Nucleolus) Dense dark-staining region within the nucleus that synthesizes ribosomal RNA (rRNA).
Mitochondria The 'powerhouses of the cell'; generates ATP energy via aerobic cellular respiration.
Ribosomes Protein factories composed of rRNA; translate mRNA transcripts into polypeptide chains.
Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum Membranous network studded with ribosomes; folds and modifies nascent proteins.
Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum Agranular network for lipid and steroid synthesis, calcium storage, and drug detox.
Golgi Complex (Apparatus) Stack of flattened sacs that chemically modifies, sorts, and packages glycoproteins for secretion.
Lysosomes 'Digestive bags' packed with hydrolytic acid enzymes to recycle damaged organelles and bacteria.
Centrioles Paired barrel-shaped organelles that organize the mitotic spindle during cell division (mitosis).
Plasma Membrane Selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer regulating what enters and leaves the cytoplasm.
Microvilli Finger-like membrane folds that increase absorptive surface area (prominent in intestine/kidney).
ATLAS: 2. Integumentary System Architecture & Receptors
The Skin & Appendages (Module 3 • Chapter 6)
Epidermis Avascular superficial protective layer of stratified squamous keratinized epithelium (5 strata).
Stratum Basale Deepest epidermal monolayer of stem cells undergoing active mitosis; houses melanocytes.
Stratum Corneum Outermost layer of dead, fully keratinized, shingle-like cells providing waterproof barrier.
Dermis Vascular, tough connective tissue layer (20x thicker than epidermis) containing nerves, vessels, and fibers.
Papillary Dermis Superficial dermal layer with dermal papillae forming fingerprint ridges and housing Meissner corpuscles.
Reticular Dermis Deep dense irregular connective tissue layer rich in collagen and elastin; houses Pacinian corpuscles.
Hypodermis (Subcutaneous) Adipose tissue foundation providing thermal insulation, energy reserve, and shock cushioning.
Meissner Corpuscle Encapsulated tactile receptor in dermal papillae that detects light touch and low-frequency vibration.
Pacinian Corpuscle Large lamellated onion-shaped receptor deep in reticular dermis/hypodermis detecting deep pressure.
Free Nerve Endings Unencapsulated sensory dendrites extending into epidermis to detect nociceptive pain and temperature.
Sebaceous Glands Oil glands attached to hair follicles secreting sebum to lubricate hair/skin and prevent microbial growth.
Eccrine Sweat Glands Coiled tubular glands distributed across the body that secrete watery sweat for thermoregulation.
Arrector Pili Muscle Smooth muscle bundle contracting under sympathetic stimulation to pull hair upright ('goosebumps').
ATLAS: 3. The Complete Human Skeleton (Axial & Appendicular)
Skeletal System (Module 4 • Chapter 7)
Cranium & Facial Bones Axial: Protects brain (Frontal, Parietal, Occipital) and frames face (Maxilla, Zygomatic, Mandible).
Vertebral Column Axial: Flexible supportive column with 7 Cervical, 12 Thoracic, 5 Lumbar, Sacrum, and Coccyx.
Thoracic Cage Axial: Bony protection for heart/lungs composed of Sternum and 12 pairs of ribs (7 True, 3 False, 2 Floating).
Pectoral Girdle Appendicular: Attaches upper limbs to axial trunk; includes Clavicle (collarbone) and Scapula (shoulder blade).
Humerus Appendicular: Long bone of the upper arm articulating with glenoid cavity and elbow.
Radius & Ulna Appendicular: Forearm bones; Radius is lateral (thumb side) and Ulna is medial (pinky side).
Carpals, Metacarpals, Phalanges Appendicular: Wrist (8 carpals), palm (5 metacarpals), and fingers (14 phalanges).
Pelvic Girdle (Coxal Bone) Appendicular: Sturdy weight-bearing ring formed by fused Ilium, Ischium, and Pubis bones.
Femur Appendicular: Heaviest, strongest, and longest bone in the body; articulates with acetabulum at hip.
Patella Appendicular: Sesamoid kneecap protecting the knee joint and improving quadriceps leverage.
Tibia & Fibula Appendicular: Lower leg; Tibia (shinbone) is medial and weight-bearing; Fibula is lateral stabilizer.
Tarsals, Metatarsals, Calcaneus Appendicular: Ankle (7 tarsals), heel (calcaneus), arch (5 metatarsals), and toes (14 phalanges).
ATLAS: 4. Major Superficial Skeletal Muscles (Anterior & Posterior)
Muscular System (Module 5 • Chapter 8)
Sternocleidomastoid Neck muscle that flexes neck and rotates head to opposite side.
Trapezius Large triangular upper back/neck muscle that elevates, depresses, and retracts the scapula.
Deltoid Triangular shoulder muscle that serves as the prime mover of arm abduction.
Pectoralis Major Large chest muscle that adducts, flexes, and medially rotates the humerus at the shoulder.
Biceps Brachii Anterior arm muscle that is the prime mover of elbow flexion and powerful forearm supinator.
Triceps Brachii Posterior arm muscle that acts as the primary antagonist to biceps, extending the elbow joint.
Rectus Abdominis 'Six-pack' abdominal muscle that flexes the lumbar vertebral column and compresses abdominal cavity.
External Obliques Lateral abdominal muscles that rotate and laterally flex the trunk.
Latissimus Dorsi 'Swimmer's muscle'; broad lower back muscle that extends and adducts the arm.
Gluteus Maximus Largest muscle in the body; prime mover of hip extension (rising from sitting, climbing stairs).
Quadriceps Femoris Group Anterior thigh muscles (Rectus femoris, Vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius) extending the knee.
Hamstrings Group Posterior thigh muscles (Biceps femoris, Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus) flexing knee and extending hip.
Tibialis Anterior Anterior shin muscle that performs ankle dorsiflexion and foot inversion.
Gastrocnemius & Soleus Posterior calf muscles that plantarflex the ankle via the strong Calcaneal (Achilles) tendon.
ATLAS: 5. The Human Endocrine System (Master Glands & Hormones)
Endocrine System (Module 8 • Chapter 11)
Pineal Gland Located in brain epithalamus; secretes Melatonin in darkness to coordinate circadian sleep-wake cycles.
Hypothalamus Master neuroendocrine coordinator; synthesizes ADH and Oxytocin; controls anterior pituitary via releasing hormones.
Pituitary Gland (Master Gland) Anterior lobe secretes TSH, ACTH, GH, PRL, FSH, LH; Posterior lobe stores and releases ADH & Oxytocin.
Thyroid Gland Butterfly-shaped neck gland; secretes T3 & T4 (metabolic rate) and Calcitonin (lowers blood calcium).
Parathyroid Glands Four tiny glands on posterior thyroid; secrete Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) to elevate blood calcium levels.
Thymus Gland Located behind sternum; secretes Thymosin to program and mature immunocompetent T-lymphocytes.
Adrenal Glands (Suprarenal) Cortex secretes Aldosterone (Na+ retention) and Cortisol (glucose/stress); Medulla secretes Epinephrine.
Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans) Beta cells secrete Insulin (lowers blood glucose); Alpha cells secrete Glucagon (elevates blood glucose).
Ovaries (Female) Secrete Estrogen (secondary sex characteristics) and Progesterone (menstrual cycle & pregnancy support).
Testes (Male) Secrete Testosterone (spermatogenesis, muscle mass, secondary male sex characteristics).
