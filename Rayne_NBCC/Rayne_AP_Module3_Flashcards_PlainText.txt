PART 1: SKIN LAYERS & CELL TYPES

FLASHCARD 1
FRONT: What is the Epidermis and its vascularity?
BACK: The outermost layer of skin composed of stratified squamous epithelium. It is nonvascular (has no blood vessels) and receives nutrients via diffusion from the dermis below.

FLASHCARD 2
FRONT: What are the 5 Layers of the Epidermis (Deep to Surface)?
BACK:
1. Stratum Basale: Deepest layer; active cell division (mitosis).
2. Stratum Spinosum: Spiny layer; cellular strength.
3. Stratum Granulosum: Granular layer; keratin production begins.
4. Stratum Lucidum: Clear layer found ONLY in thick skin (palms, soles, lips).
5. Stratum Corneum: Outermost layer of dead, flat, keratinized cells.

FLASHCARD 3
FRONT: Keratinocytes vs Melanocytes
BACK:
Keratinocytes (95% of cells): Produce tough fibrous protein keratin that waterproofs skin and prevents dehydration.
Melanocytes (5% of cells): Produce pigment melanin that absorbs UV radiation and determines skin color.

FLASHCARD 4
FRONT: What is the Dermis and its 2 Layers?
BACK: Middle layer 20x thicker than epidermis, packed with collagen and elastic fibers, blood vessels, and nerves.
Papillary Layer: Contains dermal papillae (fingerprints) and pain/touch receptors.
Reticular Layer: Deeper thick layer containing collagen/elastin, glands, hair follicles, and Pacinian pressure receptors.

FLASHCARD 5
FRONT: What is the Hypodermis (Subcutaneous Tissue)?
BACK: Foundation layer under skin composed mainly of adipose (fat) tissue. Functions: energy storage, thermal insulation, shock absorption/cushioning, and attaching skin to underlying muscle/bone.


PART 2: ACCESSORY ORGANS (HAIR, NAILS, GLANDS)

FLASHCARD 6
FRONT: What are the 3 Components of Hair & Arrector Pili Muscle?
BACK:
Hair Shaft: Visible part above skin.
Hair Root: Hidden portion inside follicle.
Hair Papilla/Bulb: Cap-shaped cluster of blood-nourished cells at base where growth begins.
Arrector Pili Muscle: Smooth muscle that contracts when cold/frightened to cause "goosebumps".

FLASHCARD 7
FRONT: What are the 3 Components of a Nail & Cyanosis?
BACK:
Nail Body: Visible plate made of hardened keratin.
Nail Root: Hidden under cuticle (eponychium).
Lunula: Crescent-shaped white area near root.
Cyanosis: Bluish discoloration of nail beds due to poor blood oxygen perfusion.

FLASHCARD 8
FRONT: Eccrine vs Apocrine Sweat Glands
BACK:
Eccrine Glands: Most abundant all over body; produce thin watery perspiration for temperature regulation and waste removal.
Apocrine Glands: Found in axilla, groin, nipples; produce thicker secretion that creates body odor when broken down by skin bacteria.

FLASHCARD 9
FRONT: What are Sebaceous Glands & Sebum?
BACK: Oil glands attached to hair follicles that secrete sebum ("nature's skin cream") to lubricate skin and prevent drying/cracking. Blocked ducts form whiteheads (pimples) or blackheads (oxidized sebum).


PART 3: CUTANEOUS MECHANORECEPTORS (SENSATION)

FLASHCARD 10
FRONT: Meissner's Corpuscle
BACK: Mechanoreceptor located in dermal papillae responsible for sensing texture and light vibration / touch.

FLASHCARD 11
FRONT: Pacinian Corpuscle
BACK: Deep mechanoreceptor located in reticular dermis/hypodermis responsible for sensing deep pressure and fast vibration.

FLASHCARD 12
FRONT: Ruffini's End Organ
BACK: Mechanoreceptor responsible for detecting skin stretch.

FLASHCARD 13
FRONT: End Bulb of Krause
BACK: Cutaneous receptor specialized for detecting cold temperatures.

FLASHCARD 14
FRONT: Merkel's Disc
BACK: Sensory receptor responsible for detecting sustained touch and pressure.

FLASHCARD 15
FRONT: Free Nerve Endings
BACK: Unencapsulated nerve endings throughout skin that signal pain and itching.


PART 4: 6 CORE FUNCTIONS OF THE SKIN

FLASHCARD 16
FRONT: How does the skin provide Protection?
BACK: Acts as body's first line of defense against microbial invasion, UV radiation, chemical exposure, physical cuts, and dehydration.

FLASHCARD 17
FRONT: How does the skin regulate Temperature?
BACK:
Heat Loss: Sweating (evaporative cooling) and dermal vasodilation.
Heat Retention: Dermal vasoconstriction, shivering, and arrector pili muscle contraction (goosebumps).

FLASHCARD 18
FRONT: How does the skin function in Excretion?
BACK: Eliminates metabolic waste products (urea, uric acid, ammonia, salts, water) through sweat gland activity.

FLASHCARD 19
FRONT: How does the skin synthesize Vitamin D?
BACK: UV light exposure converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin cells into Vitamin D precursors, which are subsequently activated by enzymes in the liver and kidneys.

FLASHCARD 20
FRONT: How does the skin function in Absorption?
BACK: Absorbs lipid-soluble substances via passive diffusion (example: transdermal medication patches like nitroglycerin, nicotine, hormone creams, as well as lipid toxins).
