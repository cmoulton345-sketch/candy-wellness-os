## Module 1: Basic Communication

### Flashcard 1
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Communication?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** A complex, ongoing two-way process of sharing ideas, thoughts, and feelings. It involves a sender encoding a message, transmitting through a channel, a receiver decoding the message, and providing feedback to complete the cycle.

---

### Flashcard 2
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Nonverbal Communication & why is it crucial?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Physical gestures, facial expressions, body posture, eye contact, tone, pitch, and proxemics. It accounts for **80–93%** of face-to-face communication impact. When nonverbal cues contradict verbal words, people trust the nonverbal cues!

---

### Flashcard 3
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are the 4 Zones of Proxemics (Space)?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  1. **Intimate Space:** 0 to 45 cm (0–18 in) — Close emotional ties or physical care.
  2. **Personal Distance:** 45 cm to 1.2 m (18 in–3.5 ft) — Arm's reach; ideal for history taking.
  3. **Social Distance:** 1.2 m to 3.6 m (3.5–12 ft) — Formal / business relationships.
  4. **Public Distance:** 3.6 m+ (12 ft+) — Out in public / non-personal settings.

---

### Flashcard 4
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Metacommunication?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Broad term for all factors (verbal and nonverbal cues, posture, eye contact, expressions) that provide instructions on **how to interpret** the message being delivered.

---

### Flashcard 5
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Congruent Communication vs Incongruent Communication?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Congruent:** Spoken words and nonverbal body language match perfectly (e.g., patient says "I am relaxed" and sits calmly).
  * **Incongruent:** Words and nonverbal body language conflict (e.g., patient says "No pain" while grimacing and guarding abdomen). *Nurse must explore incongruence!*

---

### Flashcard 6
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Social Communication vs Professional Therapeutic Communication
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Social:** Casual conversation with peers/family, unwritten rules, driven by mutual pleasure & self-interest.
  * **Professional Therapeutic:** Goal-directed, purposeful, evidence-based clinical interaction bound by legal/ethical standards to promote patient health & safety.

## Module 2: Effective Communication (Bridges)

### Flashcard 7
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Respect in Nursing Communication?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Valuing patient knowledge, opinions, and dignity. Always address patients by their preferred name. Avoid patronizing terms like "Honey," "Dear," or "Sweetie," and avoid asking "How are *we* feeling today?"

---

### Flashcard 8
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are Roach's 6 C's of Caring?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  1. **Compassion** (feeling with the patient)
  2. **Competence** (knowledge & skill)
  3. **Confidence** (fostering trust)
  4. **Conscience** (ethical integrity)
  5. **Commitment** (pledge to care)
  6. **Comportment** (professional demeanor)

---

### Flashcard 9
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Swanson's Caring Theory (1991)?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Knowing:** Understanding the meaning of an event in the patient's life.
  * **Being:** Emotionally present and sharing feelings.
  * **Doing:** Performing nursing interventions to preserve integrity.

---

### Flashcard 10
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Mutuality in Nursing?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Collaborative relationship where nurse and patient share decision-making, agree on health goals, and work as equal partners. Respects patient autonomy even if choices differ from nurse's opinion.

---

### Flashcard 11
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Empowerment vs Disempowerment?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Empowerment:** Assisting patients to take charge of their lives by providing tools, education, and confidence for self-management.
  * **Disempowerment:** Demonstrating a paternalistic "I know best" attitude that strips patient autonomy and choice.

---

### Flashcard 12
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What is Empathy vs Sympathy?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Empathy:** Ability to put oneself in another's shoes, understanding their perspective nonjudgmentally while keeping professional objectivity.
  * **Sympathy:** Feeling pity or sorrow for someone, which can blur clinical judgment and boundaries.

---

## Module 3: Barriers to Effective Communication

### Flashcard 13
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Why is False Reassurance a major communication barrier?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Saying "Don't worry, everything will be fine" offers ungrounded hope, invalidates genuine patient fears, destroys trust, and blocks the patient from expressing true feelings.

---

### Flashcard 14
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Bias vs Stereotype — What is the difference?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  * **Bias:** A personal preference, like, or dislike that interferes with impartiality.
  * **Stereotype:** A fixed, preconceived idea attributing specific characteristics to *all* members of a category or group.

---

### Flashcard 15
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Why is Giving Advice / Opinions non-therapeutic?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Saying "If I were you..." takes away patient decision-making and fosters dependence. Therapeutic alternative: **Reflecting** (e.g., "What options are you considering?") to empower patient decision-making.

---

### Flashcard 16
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** Why is Arguing with a patient harmful?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** Challenging or arguing patient perceptions implies they are lying or misinformed, destroys rapport, displays unprofessional behavior, and escalates conflict instead of resolving underlying anxiety.

---

### Flashcard 17
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** How does Anxiety affect patient communication?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):** High anxiety causes distracted thinking, hyper-focus on negative cues, overthinking sentences, and impaired listening/comprehension. Nurses must respond with calm, slow, unhurried, clear statements.

---

### Flashcard 18
* **FRONT (Term / Question):** What are 4 Key Nursing Strategies to Reduce Patient Anxiety?
* **BACK (Definition & Meaning):**
  1. Active listening & validating feelings.
  2. Honest, simple explanations of procedures/tests.
  3. Calm, slow, firm, unhurried communication.
  4. Relaxation techniques (deep breathing, guided imagery, touch when appropriate).
