### Part 2: True / False Answers & Rationales

1. **TRUE** — *Rationale:* Research (Arnold & Boggs; O'Daniel & Rosenstein) confirms that improper communication among healthcare teams is the leading root cause of preventable adverse events and medical errors.
2. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* When verbal and nonverbal messages conflict (incongruence), receivers consistently trust the **nonverbal body language and expressions** over spoken words.
3. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* Nonverbal cues (body posture, facial expression, tone of voice) account for up to **80–93%** of face-to-face communication effectiveness. Tone of voice alone accounts for 38%.
4. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* Social communication is casual and unwritten, while **professional therapeutic communication** is goal-directed, structured, and governed by ethical/legal boundaries.
5. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* Terms like "Honey," "Dear," or "Sweetie" are patronizing and diminish a patient's sense of dignity and personal control.
6. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* Paternalistic "I know what's best for you" attitudes cause **disempowerment**, creating passivity and poorer self-management outcomes.
7. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* Empathy involves understanding and validating the patient's feelings while maintaining professional boundaries and nonjudgmental stance.
8. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* False reassurance destroys trust, dismisses real fears, and prevents patients from expressing genuine anxieties.
9. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* It is the reverse! A **bias** is a personal preference or dislike, whereas a **stereotype** is a generalized characteristic attributed to an entire group.
10. **FALSE** — *Rationale:* For anxious patients, nurses should speak **clearly, slowly, and calmly** in an unhurried manner, breaking down instructions into manageable steps.

### Part 3: Multiple Choice Answers & Rationales

1. **C** — *Rationale:* The patient exhibits **incongruent communication** (verbal says "no pain," nonverbal shows grimacing/guarding). Nonverbal cues take precedence, and the nurse must explore the discrepancy.
2. **B** — *Rationale:* **Personal distance** (45 cm to 1.2 meters) is ideal for conducting personal clinical interviews while keeping the patient within arm's reach comfortably.
3. **B** — *Rationale:* Professional communication is uniquely defined by health-related goals, therapeutic purpose, and legal/ethical boundaries.
4. **B** — *Rationale:* Joint Commission and nursing research identify communication breakdown as the top root cause for sentinel events, medication errors, and treatment delays.
5. **A** — *Rationale:* Sister Simone Roach's 6 C's of Caring are **Compassion, Competence, Confidence, Conscience, Commitment, and Comportment**.
6. **C** — *Rationale:* Partnering with the patient, respecting their expertise in their own life, and encouraging self-management demonstrates **mutuality and empowerment**.
7. **C** — *Rationale:* Trust is built on consistency, truthfulness, follow-through on promises, and absolute respect for confidentiality.
8. **B** — *Rationale:* Swanson's Mid-Range Theory of Caring defines caring through **Knowing** (understanding patient needs), **Being** (emotionally present), and **Doing** (competent interventions).
9. **B** — *Rationale:* Stating "Everything will be fine" is **false reassurance**, which minimizes patient distress and shuts down therapeutic dialogue.
10. **B** — *Rationale:* Attributing generalized traits to an entire category of people (e.g., elderly patients) is the definition of **stereotyping**.
11. **D** — *Rationale:* Giving personal advice or opinions is non-therapeutic. Reflecting and exploring options empowers the family to make their own decision.
12. **B** — *Rationale:* High anxiety consumes cognitive bandwidth, leading to distracted thinking, hyper-focus on negative cues, overthinking, and difficulty processing incoming information.
