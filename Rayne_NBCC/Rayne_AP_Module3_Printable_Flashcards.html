A&P MODULE 3CARD #1
Q: What is the Epidermis and its vascularity?
A: Outermost layer composed of stratified squamous epithelium. It is nonvascular (no blood vessels) and receives nutrients via diffusion from the underlying dermis.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #2
Q: What are the 5 Epidermal Layers (Deep to Surface)?
1. Stratum Basale: Deepest layer; active mitosis.
2. Stratum Spinosum: Cellular strength.
3. Stratum Granulosum: Keratin production.
4. Stratum Lucidum: Found ONLY in thick skin.
5. Stratum Corneum: Outermost dead keratinized cells.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #3
Q: Keratinocytes vs Melanocytes
Keratinocytes (95%): Produce tough waterproof protein keratin.
Melanocytes (5%): Produce pigment melanin to absorb UV light and give skin color.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #4
Q: What is the Dermis and its 2 Layers?
Middle connective layer 20x thicker than epidermis.
• Papillary Layer: Dermal papillae (fingerprints) & touch receptors.
• Reticular Layer: Collagen, elastin, glands, hair follicles, & Pacinian corpuscles.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #5
Q: What is the Hypodermis (Subcutaneous Tissue)?
A: Foundation layer beneath skin composed mainly of adipose (fat) tissue. Functions: energy storage, thermal insulation, shock absorption, and attaching skin to underlying tissues.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #6
Q: 3 Components of Hair & Arrector Pili Muscle
• Shaft: Visible part.
• Root: Hidden in follicle.
• Papilla/Bulb: Blood-nourished base where growth begins.
• Arrector Pili: Muscle that contracts for "goosebumps".
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #7
Q: 3 Components of a Nail & Cyanosis
• Nail Body: Visible plate of hardened keratin.
• Nail Root: Hidden under cuticle.
• Lunula: White crescent near root.
• Cyanosis: Bluish nail beds from poor oxygen blood perfusion.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #8
Q: Eccrine vs Apocrine Sweat Glands
Eccrine Glands: Abundant all over body; thin watery sweat for cooling and waste removal.
Apocrine Glands: Axilla & groin; thicker fluid producing odor when acted on by skin bacteria.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #9
Q: Sebaceous Glands & Sebum
A: Oil glands attached to hair follicles that secrete sebum to prevent drying. Blocked ducts form whiteheads (pimples) or blackheads (oxidized sebum).
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #10
Q: Meissner's Corpuscle
A: Cutaneous mechanoreceptor located in dermal papillae responsible for sensing texture and light vibration / touch.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #11
Q: Pacinian Corpuscle
A: Deep mechanoreceptor located in reticular dermis/hypodermis responsible for sensing deep pressure and fast vibration.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #12
Q: Ruffini's End Organ
A: Sensory mechanoreceptor responsible for detecting skin stretch.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)
A&P MODULE 3CARD #13
Q: End Bulb of Krause
A: Cutaneous thermoreceptor specialized for detecting cold temperatures.
Anatomy & Physiology (HCSS1087A)

Q: Merkel's Disc & Free Nerve Endings
• Merkel's Disc: Sustained touch and pressure.
• Free Nerve Endings: Unencapsulated nerve endings signaling pain and itch.

2. Temp Regulation (sweating & shivering)
3. Sensation (tactile & temperature)
4. Excretion (sweat & urea)
5. Vitamin D Synthesis (UV light → liver/kidney)
6. Absorption (transdermal patches)
