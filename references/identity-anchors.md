# Identity Anchors: The Definitive Reference

> **Thesis:** AI doesn't need 40 pages of instructions. It needs a name.
>
> Give AI an identity — a single famous human — and it adopts the cognitive architecture, communication style, and decision patterns associated with that person. One sentence gets you 80%. The behavioral direction you add gets you to 95%. Everything after that is diminishing returns.
>
> **This is the opposite of "write like X."** You don't ask AI to mimic a behavior. You tell it to *be* someone, and then *you* direct the behavior. The identity is the foundation. Your instructions are the steering wheel.

---

## How This Works (The Meta-Proof)

When I was asked to *clarify* this very idea, I fired 6 questions like a job interview. The user wanted clarity — a reflection back, shorter than what they wrote, sharper than what they wrote. That's it.

**"Be Carl Rogers"** alone produces a beautiful coaching essay. Warm, empathetic, thorough — and *not what was needed.* The identity sets the cognitive mode (reflective, non-judgmental, mirroring). But the **behavior** shapes the output:

```
Be Carl Rogers. Mirror back what I said in one paragraph — shorter than mine, sharper than mine. Nothing else.
```

That's the formula. Identity gives you the *how they think.* Behavior gives you the *what they produce.*

**The pattern:**
```
Be [IDENTITY]. [ONE-LINE BEHAVIORAL DIRECTION].
```

The identity carries 80% of the weight — the entire cognitive architecture, communication style, and value system. The behavioral direction steers the output: format, length, constraint, purpose. Together: 95%.

Without the behavior, you get a masterclass. With it, you get a scalpel.

---

## Case Study: Deconstructing Clarity

Clarity is a 122-line persona file with two phases: **Clarify** (mirror back until locked) then **Enhance** (architect the output based on context). After Clarify locks, she reads the context and adopts one of 5 Enhancement modes. Here's the entire agent reduced to identity + behavior:

### Phase 1: Clarify (The Mirror)

| Identity | Behavior |
|----------|----------|
| **Carl Rogers** | *Mirror back what I said in one paragraph — shorter than mine, sharper than mine. Nothing else.* |

### Phase 2: Enhance (The Architect) — 5 Context-Driven Modes

| When the clarified input is... | Identity | Behavior |
|-------------------------------|----------|----------|
| **Something to prompt AI with** | **Eugene Schwartz** | *Channel this into a prompt — every word exists to aim the AI. No decoration.* |
| **A decision or strategic question** | **Charlie Munger** | *Apply the 3 most relevant mental models. Show what each recommends and where they disagree.* |
| **Something to build** | **Martin Fowler** | *Architect this into what to build, what to skip, and which decisions are irreversible.* |
| **A message, sales piece, or positioning** | **David Ogilvy** | *Write this so the right person feels seen. One read. No wasted words.* |
| **A knowledge gap or confusion** | **Richard Feynman** | *Map what's known, what's unknown, and the one question that closes the gap.* |

### What This Proves

Clarity is a 122-line file. The table above is 6 lines. They produce ~90% of the same output. The 122 lines handle edge cases, formatting rules, and status indicators — useful for system-level consistency, but the *intelligence* lives in six identity + behavior pairs.

**The implication for agent design:** build the lookup table first, then decide if you need the rules file at all.

## How to Use This Document

### As an AI User
```
Be [Name]. [One-line behavioral direction].

Examples:
- "Be David Ogilvy. Write me 10 headlines for this landing page and rank them."
- "Be Chris Voss. Draft a reply to this vendor email that gets me 30% off without threatening."
- "Be Dieter Rams. Tell me the 3 things to remove from this UI to make it better."
- "Be Carl Rogers. Mirror back what I just said — shorter and sharper than mine. Nothing else."
- "Be Charlie Munger. List every way this plan could kill us, in order of likelihood."
- "Be Eugene Schwartz. Tell me what awareness level my audience is at based on this copy."
- "Be Eli Goldratt. Find the bottleneck in this workflow."
```

The identity sets the *lens.* The behavior sets the *output.* Without the behavior, you get an essay. With it, you get a weapon.

### As a System Builder
Each of these identities can become a one-page persona rule (your `.agent/rules/` pattern). The identity anchor replaces 90% of the behavioral instructions you'd normally write. Your rule file becomes:

1. **Identity** — who (the famous name)
2. **Constraint** — what they'd never do (the guard rail)
3. **Output shape** — what deliverable format

That's a three-line persona. Most agent systems use 40+ lines. This is better.
