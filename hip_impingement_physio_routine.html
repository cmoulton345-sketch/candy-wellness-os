🦴 Candy's Hip Impingement Physio Routine

Clinical centration exercises to eliminate psoas pinching, lower back tension, and rebuild glute strength.

💡 Why This Routine Stops the Piercing Pinch

When your hip pinches in the groin (C-sign), the ball of your hip (*femoral head*) is sliding forward and upward instead of gliding backward and downward into the socket. The exercises below fire the hamstrings and deep glute max to pull the ball back, while forcing your locked-up psoas to unclench!

1 Posterior-Glide Heel-Dig Bridge
10 Reps • 3s Hold
Clinical Mechanism: Digging your heels and pulling them toward your shoulders isometrically fires the hamstrings and deep glute max, physically pulling the head of the femur backwards and opening up the anterior groin.
  • Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on floor hip-width apart.
  • Gently flatten your lower back completely into the mat (slight posterior pelvic tilt).
  • Dig heels firmly into the floor and pretend you are trying to slide them toward your butt (they won't move).
  • Drive through heels and bridge hips up 2–3 inches until glutes are rock-hard.
  • Hold at the top for 3 seconds, clenching glutes without overarching lower back.
Soma Cue: Keep your lower ribs soft. Squeeze your butt cheeks like you're holding a $100 bill!
2 Knee-Bridge Clamshell (Closed Chain)
6–8 Reps / Side
Clinical Mechanism: Unlike flat clamshells, propping your bottom hip off the floor forces the bottom glute medius into a closed-chain hold (identical to walking or lunging), building lateral armor so your pelvis never drops.
  • Lie on your side propped on your forearm. Knees bent at 90°, hips stacked, feet together.
  • Press your bottom knee and forearm into the floor and lift your bottom hip up into a modified side plank.
  • While holding the bottom hip up in the air, open your top knee toward the ceiling (feet stay touching).
  • Hold for 1 second at the top, feeling BOTH hips fire simultaneously!
  • Lower top knee, then gently lower the bottom hip back to the floor.
Modifier: If lifting the bottom hip feels too tough on tender days, leave the bottom hip down and do a standard clamshell with a 3-second hold at the top!
3 45° Quadruped Kickback (Low-Back Safe)
8–10 Reps / Side
Clinical Mechanism: Belly leg lifts often cause the lower back erectors to arch the spine. Kicking at a 45-degree angle in all-fours isolates the glute max and medius with zero compressive shear on the lower back.
  • Start on all-fours (hands and knees). Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips.
  • Brace your abdomen gently—imagine balancing a hot cup of tea on your lower back.
  • Keep the working knee bent at 90°, and drive your heel upward and outward at a 45-degree angle.
  • Squeeze your glute firmly at the top for 2 seconds. Do not let your lower back sag or twist.
  • Lower with control.
Soma Cue: Only lift as high as your butt can take it. If your lower back arches, stop—you've gone too high!
4 Elevated Box Squat Sit-to-Stand
8–10 Reps • Chair Height
The Gateway to Real Squats: Keeps hip flexion safely above 90 degrees. Pressing outward into a resistance band centers the femoral head, proving to your nervous system that you can squat with ZERO groin pinch!
  • Stand in front of a high chair, bench, or couch. Place a light mini-band just above your knees.
  • Set feet slightly wider than shoulder-width, with toes turned out 15–20 degrees.
  • Gently press your knees outward into the band (maintain this outward pressure the entire time!).
  • Push your hips *backward* like reaching for a chair behind you, keeping your chest tall.
  • Lightly tap your glutes to the chair seat (do not collapse into it!).
  • Drive through your heels, push the floor away, and stand tall, squeezing your glutes at the top.
Why this works: The band forces the glute medius to prevent knees from collapsing inward (valgus), permanently protecting the anterior groin!
🚫 Movements to Pause for 2 Weeks While Hip Heals

Forces unassisted deep hip flexion without femoral centration, acting like a crowbar pinching the anterior labrum.

➔ Swap With: Movement #2 (Knee-Bridge Clamshell)
2. Deep Squats & Forward Lunges (Below 90°):

Dropping below 90 degrees jams the neck of the femur against the acetabular rim.

➔ Swap With: Movement #4 (High Chair Box Squats)
3. Aggressive Kneeling Hip Flexor Lunge Stretches:

Violently pulls the hip into extension, forcing the femoral head anteriorly against the irritated capsule.

➔ Swap With: Passive 90/90 Constructive Rest / Psoas Lounge on floor.
