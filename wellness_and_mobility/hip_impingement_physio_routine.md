# 🦴 Candy's 8-Minute Hip Impingement, C-Sign & Lower Back Physio Routine
**Target Areas:** Anterior Hip Capsule, Psoas / Groin Pinch, Glute Activation, SI Joint & Lower Back Decompression  
**Clinical Goals:** Re-center the femoral head posteriorly in the acetabulum, reciprocally inhibit tight psoas, build closed-chain glute armor, and bridge safely toward pain-free squats and lunges.  
**Prescribed by:** Soma | Perimenopause Movement & Mobility Specialist

## 🔍 The Clinical Biomechanics: Why Does Your Hip Pinch?

### What is the "C-Sign" Pinch?
When you cup your hand in a "C" shape over the side and front of your hip crease, you are identifying **anterior femoroacetabular irritation**. 

1. **The Anterior Glide Issue:**  
   When your hip bends (squatting, lunging, cycling, or straight-leg kicks), the ball of your hip (femoral head) is supposed to glide **backward and downward** into the deepest pocket of your socket (*acetabulum*). If the deep glutes and posterior stabilizers are sleepy, the ball slides **forward and upward**, mechanically pinching the sensitive anterior labrum, capsule, and psoas tendon against the pelvic bone.
2. **The Psoas Defensive Spasm:**  
   Your psoas tendon feels like a tight, piercing guitar string not because it's "short," but because it is **clamping down in a panic** to prevent the hip ball from sliding out of the socket.
3. **The Lower Back Connection:**  
   Because the psoas attaches directly to the front of your lumbar vertebrae (L1–L5), a chronically clamped psoas yanks the lower spine forward into excessive arching, compressing the lumbar discs and jamming the sacroiliac (SI) joint.
4. **The Fix:**  
   We do **not** aggressively stretch the hip flexor (which jams the femoral head further forward into the pinch!). Instead, we **strengthen the posterior hip sling (glutes & hamstrings)** to physically pull the femoral head back into the socket and neurologically tell the psoas to shut off via *reciprocal inhibition*.

## ⏱️ The Daily 8-Minute Physio Routine (4 Core Movements)

Do this **once daily** (morning, before a workout, or in the evening). No heavy weights required—just a yoga mat and an optional light mini-loop resistance band.

---

### Movement 1: The "Posterior-Glide" Glute Bridge with Heel Dig
* **Target:** Gluteus maximus & deep hamstrings. Mechanically draws the femoral head backwards.
* **Setup:** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart. Optional: Place a light mini-band just above your knees.
* **Execution:**
  1. Gently tilt your pelvis backward so your lower back flattens completely against the mat (*posterior pelvic tilt*).
  2. Dig your heels into the floor and **isometrically pull your heels toward your shoulders** (they will not move, but your hamstrings will instantly wake up!).
  3. Drive through your heels and lift your hips 2 to 3 inches off the floor until your glutes are fully clenched.
  4. **Hold at the top for 3 full seconds**, breathing into your belly. Squeeze your glutes like you are holding a $100 bill between your cheeks!
  5. Lower down with control.
* **Prescription:** **10 reps (3-second hold at the top of each rep)**.
* **Why this heals your pinch:** That isometric heel-dig engages the hamstrings, which creates a posterior glide force on the femur, instantly taking pressure off the anterior groin!

---

### Movement 2: The Modified Side-Plank "Knee Bridge" Clamshell
* **Target:** Gluteus medius & minimus (lateral pelvic stabilizers).
* **Why it's better than flat clamshells:** Flat clamshells are "open chain." This physio progression places the **bottom hip in a closed chain**, building the exact lateral hip strength you need for walking, climbing stairs, and lunging without your pelvis wobbling!
* **Setup:** Lie on your side propped up on your elbow. Bend both knees to 90 degrees with your hips stacked and feet together behind you.
* **Execution:**
  1. Press your bottom forearm and bottom knee into the floor, and **lift your bottom hip up off the mat** into a side plank from the knees.
  2. While holding the bottom hip up in the air, **open your top knee toward the ceiling** (keeping feet touching).
  3. Pause for 1 second at the top, feeling both glutes fire simultaneously!
  4. Lower the top knee, then gently lower the bottom hip back down.
* **Prescription:** **6 to 8 reps per side**.
* **Pain-Adaptive Cue:** If lifting the bottom hip feels too intense on tender days, keep the bottom hip on the floor and perform a standard clamshell with a 3-second squeeze at the top!

---

### Movement 3: The 45-Degree Quadruped Glute Kickback (Low-Back Safe)
* **Target:** Isolated glute max activation without hyperextending the lumbar spine.
* **Why belly leg lifts were hurting:** Lying on the belly and lifting the leg often causes the lower back muscles to fire before the glute, arching the spine and triggering lower back pain.
* **Setup:** On all fours (hands and knees). Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips. Place a folded blanket or pad under knees for comfort.
* **Execution:**
  1. Brace your core lightly, as if you are balancing a hot cup of tea on your lower back.
  2. Keep your working knee bent at 90 degrees.
  3. Instead of kicking straight back or straight out, **drive your heel upward and outward at a 45-degree angle**.
  4. Squeeze the glute firmly at the top for 2 seconds. **Do not let your lower back sag or rotate.**
  5. Lower the knee back to hovering just above the mat.
* **Prescription:** **8 to 10 reps per side**.
* **Physio Tip:** Only lift the leg as high as your glute can take it. If your lower back arches, you've gone too high!

---

### Movement 4: The Elevated "Box Squat" Sit-to-Stand (The Squat Bridge)
* **Target:** Retraining the squat pattern safely above 90 degrees with ZERO groin pinch.
* **Setup:** Stand in front of a high chair, bench, or sofa (seat height should keep your hip crease slightly *above* your knee). Place a light mini-band around your knees.
* **Execution:**
  1. Set your feet slightly wider than shoulder-width, with your **toes turned out 15 to 20 degrees** (this gives the femoral head room to clear the socket rim).
  2. Gently push your knees outward into the band. **Keep this outward tension throughout the entire movement.**
  3. Push your hips *backward* like you are reaching for a chair behind you, keeping your chest tall.
  4. Lightly tap your glutes to the chair (do not collapse into it!).
  5. Drive through your heels, push the floor away, and stand tall, squeezing your glutes at the top.
* **Prescription:** **8 to 10 slow, controlled reps**.
* **Why this works:** The resistance band forces your glute medius to fire, which prevents the knees from collapsing inward (valgus collapse), permanently eliminating that sharp groin pinch!

## 🚫 Exercises to Pause for the Next 2 Weeks

While your anterior hip capsule is cooling down, temporarily swap out these specific movements:

1. **Pause Side-Lying Straight-Leg Kicks to the Front:**  
   * *Why:* Kicking a straight leg to the front forces deep, unassisted hip flexion. If the femur isn't centrated, it acts like a crowbar pinching the anterior capsule.  
   * *Swap:* Replace with Movement #2 (Knee Bridge Clamshell) or gentle side-lying clamshells.
2. **Pause Deep Squats & Deep Lunges (Below 90°):**  
   * *Why:* Dropping below 90 degrees causes the femoral neck to contact the acetabular rim.  
   * *Swap:* Use Movement #4 (Elevated Box Squats to a chair) and shallow step-backs.
3. **Pause Aggressive Kneeling Lunge Hip-Flexor Stretches:**  
   * *Why:* Forcing the hip into extreme passive extension pushes the femoral head anteriorly against the irritated labrum.  
   * *Swap:* Use passive 90/90 Constructive Rest / Psoas Lounge on the floor.
