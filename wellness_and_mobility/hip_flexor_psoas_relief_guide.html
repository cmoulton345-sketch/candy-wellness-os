Candy’s C-Sign Hip & Psoas Reset

Anterior Femoral Glide Protocol: Why stretching worsens the pinch, how to decompress the joint, and zero-pinch workout day swaps.
🛑 The Medical Truth: Why Stretching Doesn't Help Critical Clinician Insight
Your hip flexor is NOT "tight." It is pinched because the ball of your thigh bone (femur) has drifted slightly forward in the socket.

When you feel that deep pinch in the front crease of your hip (*C-sign*), your natural instinct is to do a lunge stretch to pull the muscle back. But in an anterior femoral glide, stretching literally rams the ball of the bone harder into the inflamed front capsule and labrum. Your nervous system panics and clamps the psoas down into an even tighter defensive spasm!

👉 You cannot stretch an impingement. You must DECOMPRESS and GLIDE the bone backward.
🛋️ The 3-Step Mechanical Reset Daily & Pre-Workout
1. The Psoas Lounge (90/90 Gravity Release) 5 to 10 Minutes
Lie flat on your back on the floor or bed. Place your calves and lower legs up on a couch, chair, or stack of pillows so your hips and knees are at a relaxed 90-degree angle. Place a warm heating pad right across your front hip crease/groin. Breathe into your lower belly (4s in, 6s out).
💡 The Magic: In 90/90, hip flexors require zero effort and go completely slack. Gravity naturally traction-drops the ball of your femur backward into the center of the socket.
2. Quadruped Rock-Backs (Joint Centering) 8 to 10 Slow Reps
Come onto your hands and knees. Open your knees slightly wider than your hips with your big toes touching behind you. Keeping your back flat (do not round or arch), gently rock your hips straight back toward your heels just 3 to 4 inches. STOP 1 inch before any pinch! Pause 2s, then return.
💡 The Magic: Mechanically slides the femoral head backward into the acetabulum, clearing impingement space in the front crease.
3. Gentle Glute Bridge with Pillow Squeeze 5 Reps x 5s Hold
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor, with a small pillow between your knees. Squeeze the pillow, squeeze your butt cheeks, and lift your hips just 2 to 3 inches off the floor (low bridge—do not arch your back). Hold for 5 seconds, focusing 100% on your glutes.
💡 The Magic (Reciprocal Inhibition): When your glute muscle fires, your brain is neurologically forced to send a reflex command to the front hip flexor (psoas) to shut off its defensive spasm.
4. Posterior Wall Release (Tennis / Lacrosse Ball) 2 Minutes
Place a tennis ball or lacrosse ball into the back-outside of your hip (upper outer butt cheek / glute medius) against the wall or floor. Breathe into tender spots.
💡 The Magic: Tightness in the back-outside of the hip shoves the thigh bone forward. Softening the back wall lets the bone glide backward into place.
