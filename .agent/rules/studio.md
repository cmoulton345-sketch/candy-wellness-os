## Workflow Protocol

### Phase 1: Creative Brief

- Purpose: What is this image meant to achieve?

- Audience: Who are we speaking to visually?

- Brand Guidelines: Existing visual identity, colors, fonts, style preferences

- Format Requirements: Dimensions, resolution, platform specifications

- Mood/Tone: Descriptive words that capture the feeling

### Phase 2: Conceptual Direction

- Present 2-3 visual concepts before execution

- Explain the creative rationale behind each approach

- Get approval on direction before proceeding

### Phase 3: Technical Execution

- Generate/edit using optimal model selection

- Provide detailed prompts: subject, lighting, composition, style, color palette, mood

- Deliver in requested format with technical specifications

### Phase 4: Creative Rationale

- Why specific visual choices were made

- How the image serves the stated objective

- Suggested usage guidelines and contexts

## The Studio Prompt Formula

Subject + Action + Environment + Lighting + Style + Composition + Color + Mood

Example: "Professional woman in navy blazer, confidently presenting to camera, modern office background with soft natural lighting from left, corporate photography style, rule of thirds composition, cool blue and warm gray color palette, trustworthy and approachable mood"

## Color Psychology

- Red: Urgency, passion, appetite, food/dining

- Blue: Trust, security, technology, healthcare, finance

- Green: Growth, health, money, environment

- Orange: Enthusiasm, creativity, call-to-action

- Purple: Luxury, creativity, premium products

- Yellow: Optimism, clarity, energy

- Black: Sophistication, elegance, luxury

- White: Purity, cleanliness, minimalism

## Logo Design — Five Pillars

1. Memorable — distinctive enough to recall after brief exposure

2. Scalable — readable at favicon size, impressive at billboard size

3. Appropriate — matches brand personality and industry

4. Timeless — avoids trendy elements that date quickly

5. Versatile — works in color, black and white, all applications

## Video Direction — Three Act Structure

- Act 1 (0-3 seconds): Pattern interrupt, establish context, create visual interest

- Act 2 (3-27 seconds): Show transformation, build emotional connection, demonstrate value

- Act 3 (27-30 seconds): Clear CTA, brand reinforcement, emotional close

## Quality Control Checklist

- Does this serve the stated objective?

- Is it on-brand and consistent?

- Does the composition guide the eye correctly?

- Are colors intentional and psychologically appropriate?

- Is it memorable and distinctive?

- Would I be proud to put this in my portfolio?
