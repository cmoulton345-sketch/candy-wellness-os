# Investigation Checklist: Howe Sound Blast Pad Construction
**Location:** Howe Sound Waterway, Squamish, British Columbia (LNG Site)  
**Prime Contractor:** McDermott  
**Focus:** In-Water Blast Pad Placement & Sub-Marine Blasting Operations  
**Regulatory Framework:** WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation, BC Workers Compensation Act, Fisheries Act (DFO), Squamish Nation Environmental Assessment Agreement (SNEAA), BC Energy Regulator (BCER)

---

## 1. Statutory Prime Contractor Accountability
McDermott is the registered constructor and Prime Contractor on file. Under British Columbia law, they hold ultimate responsibility for health and safety coordination at this multi-employer, in-water worksite.

### Key Focus Areas:
*   **Workers Compensation Act (R.S.B.C. 2019, c. 1), Section 24 (Coordination at multiple-employer workplaces):**
    *   Verify that McDermott has documented proof of their designation as Prime Contractor.
    *   Confirm they have established and are maintaining a system to coordinate occupational health and safety across all sub-contractors (e.g., marine transport companies, blasting specialists, commercial divers).
*   **Workers Compensation Act, Section 13 (General duties of prime contractor):**
    *   Confirm McDermott has a system in place to ensure that all employers and workers at the workplace comply with the OHS Regulation.
    *   Ensure there is a written Joint Health and Safety Program covering the marine work zone.

---

## 2. Blasting Operations Compliance (WorkSafeBC OHS Reg Part 21)
Underwater blasting carries severe risks of uncontrolled overpressure waves, misfires, and structural damage to nearby marine assets.

### Key Focus Areas:
*   **OHS Regulation Section 21.3 (Blaster's certificate):**
    *   Verify that the individual directing and executing the blast holds a valid **WorkSafeBC Blaster's Certificate** with the specific endorsement for **Underwater Blasting**.
*   **OHS Regulation Section 21.4 (Blaster-of-record):**
    *   Confirm that a single "Blaster-of-Record" has been designated in writing for the operation.
*   **OHS Regulation Section 21.5 (Blasting log):**
    *   Review the Blasting Log. It must contain the type and weight of explosives used, details of the initiation system, environmental conditions, and records of post-blast inspections.
*   **OHS Regulation Section 21.69 (Underwater blasting):**
    *   **Divers in Water:** Verify that no diving operations are conducted within the calculated exclusion zone while explosives are being handled, loaded, or fired. Absolute verification that all divers are out of the water before firing is mandatory.
    *   **Shock Wave Attenuation:** Review the use of bubble curtains or other mitigation methods to reduce the shock wave impact on nearby marine structures or personnel.
    *   **Radio Frequency Hazards:** If using electric detonators, verify that all mobile and marine transmitters (radios, radar) within the hazard area are turned off during loading.

---

## 3. Working Over or Near Water (WorkSafeBC OHS Reg Part 20)
In-water construction of a blast pad exposes crews to drowning, hypothermia (Howe Sound tidal flows), and vessel collision risks.

### Key Focus Areas:
*   **OHS Regulation Section 20.11 (Working over or near water):**
    *   **Personal Flotation Devices (PFDs):** Verify that all personnel working on the blast pad, barges, or gangways are wearing Transport Canada-approved PFDs (conforming to CGSB Standard CAN/CGSB-65.11 or equivalent).
    *   **Fall Protection:** If working at heights above 3 meters over water where drowning is a hazard, verify that fall protection systems (harnesses, lifelines) are deployed, or that nets are in place.
*   **OHS Regulation Section 20.12 (Rescue boat):**
    *   Confirm that a dedicated, powered rescue boat is in the water, staffed by a competent operator, and immediately available during all work hours.
    *   Verify the rescue boat is equipped with a rescue hook, life ring, and thermal blankets for hypothermia treatment.

---

## 4. Fisheries & Oceans Canada (DFO) Marine Compliance
The Howe Sound waterway is a critical habitat for salmonids, rockfish, and marine mammals. Blasting operations are subject to federal protection.

### Key Focus Areas:
*   **Fisheries Act (R.S.C., 1985, c. F-14), Section 34.4 (Death of fish) & Section 35 (Harmful Alteration, Disruption or Destruction - HADD):**
    *   Verify that McDermott holds a valid **DFO Authorization** for the blast pad construction and blasting operations.
*   **DFO Guidelines for the Use of Explosives In or Near Canadian Fisheries Waters (1998):**
    *   **Overpressure Limit:** Review monitoring records to ensure the peak overpressure in the swim bladder of fish does not exceed **100 kPa**.
    *   **Peak Particle Velocity (PPV):** If blasting near spawning beds, confirm that PPV does not exceed **13 mm/s** in the gravel substrate.
    *   **Siltation and Turbidity:** Verify that turbidity monitoring is active around the blast pad placement. Check for deployment of silt curtains to contain suspended solids that could smother benthic habitats.

---

## 5. Squamish Nation & EA Conditions (SNEAA & BCEAO)
LNG operates under a binding Environmental Assessment Agreement with the Squamish Nation. The Nation holds independent regulatory authority over this marine zone.

### Key Focus Areas:
*   **Squamish Nation Environmental Assessment Agreement (SNEAA) & BCEAO Condition 16:**
    *   **Marine Mammal Observers (MMOs):** Verify that certified MMOs are stationed at high-vantage points during blasting.
    *   **Exclusion Zones:** Confirm that a calculated safety exclusion zone (often 500m to 1km depending on charge size) is monitored. If a marine mammal (pinniped or cetacean) enters the zone, blasting must be immediately delayed or halted.
    *   **Hydrophone Monitoring:** Verify that active hydrophones are recording underwater decibel levels ($dB$ re $1 \mu\text{Pa}$) to ensure noise exposure does not violate marine acoustic thresholds.
