## 2. Joe's Healthcare & Doctor Strategy: The Two Options

Managing Crohn's disease requires uninterrupted access to specialists, colonoscopy monitoring, and biologic medications (e.g., Inflectra, Remicade, Stelara). 

Here are the two pathways available for Joe, with real-world pros and cons:

---

### OPTION A (Recommended for Health & Doctor Security):
### Maintain NB Medicare as an Interprovincial "Mobile Worker"

*Because your spouse, family home, and legal ties remain in New Brunswick, you qualify as an interprovincial commuter/mobile worker temporarily stationed in BC for employment.*

* **NB Family Doctor Status:** **100% Protected.** You remain an active, funded patient on your NB family doctor's roster. You will never be de-rostered or lose your spot to the provincial waitlist.
* **Crohn's Specialist (GI):** **100% Protected.** Your NB gastroenterologist remains your primary attending specialist for annual colonoscopies, blood monitoring, and renewals during trips home.
* **Biologics & Medications:** 
  - Your new employer's private extended health plan covers your prescription drugs.
  - Coordinate with your drug's **Patient Support Program** (e.g., BioAdvance, AbbVie Care). They can set up medication delivery or infusion clinic appointments at a Vancouver clinic while your NB specialist remains the prescriber.
* **Coverage in BC:** Your NB Medicare card covers emergency, walk-in clinic, and hospital visits in BC under Canada's reciprocal medical agreement.
* **CRA Tax Alignment:** Clean and audit-proof. You file as a New Brunswick resident (matching your family home and spouse), eliminating the risk of CRA clawing back out-of-province tax adjustments.

---

### OPTION B (The BC Tax Savings Route):
### Switch to BC MSP (Medical Services Plan)

*You establish formal provincial residency in BC to take advantage of lower provincial income tax rates.*

* **NB Family Doctor Status:** **HIGH RISK.** When you register for BC MSP, NB Medicare is notified and cancels your NB card. With New Brunswick's severe doctor shortage, clinic managers routinely de-roster patients who hold out-of-province health cards. Once dropped, getting back on an NB family doctor roster is nearly impossible.
* **Crohn's Specialist (GI):** Can technically bill BC MSP under reciprocal billing when you are in NB, but cross-provincial telemedicine (phone/virtual visits) is restricted by medical licensing bodies.
* **Biologics & Medications:** Requires transferring your biologic authorization to BC PharmaCare or BC private benefits, and establishing care with a Vancouver gastroenterologist or infusion center.
* **CRA Tax Reality:** You save ~$7,500–$8,000/year in provincial taxes on a $250k salary. **However**, CRA Income Tax Folio S5-F1-C1 classifies a spouse and family home as "Primary Residential Ties." CRA may audit and deem you a factual NB resident, forcing you to repay the tax difference in the spring unless you carefully document your separate BC household.
