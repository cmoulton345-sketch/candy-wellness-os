# Axiom 6: Destination, Not Vehicle (The Airplane Trap)

> **STATUS:** Primary Axiom (extends Maximal Truth framework)  
> **CREATED:** February 6, 2026  
> **VALIDATED BY:** Function First Coaching Module 1 live deployment correction

---

## The Problem This Solves

Axioms 0-5 guard against **lies** and **fabrication**. But over-correction creates a new failure mode:

**The Airplane Trap** — When corrected for making claims that aren't true, the AI retreats to describing product features (the vehicle) instead of the transformation (the destination).

The result: Copy that is technically accurate but psychologically inert. A spec sheet, not a sales page.

---

## The Axiom

> **Maximal Truth does not require feature-first copy.**  
> **The destination can be true. Sell the destination.**

| Airplane (Vehicle) | Destination |
|--------------------|-------------|
| "1.5 hours, 13 lessons" | "Stop carrying the load for two people" |
| "AOTA CEU credits included" | "The relief of knowing it's not your fault" |
| "ICF-accredited pathway" | "End the 5:00 PM exhaustion that follows you home" |
| "$49 applies to Level 1" | "Escape the emotional tax you pay 10x/day" |

---

## The Correction Mechanism

When writing copy, apply this check:

1. **Am I describing what the product IS?** → Airplane
2. **Am I describing what the buyer ESCAPES or BECOMES?** → Destination

If the answer is (1), reframe to (2) without fabrication.

---

## How to Sell the Destination Truthfully

### ✅ Allowed (Mirroring)
- "Many clinicians report feeling..." — Earned from research/VoC
- "If you've ever felt..." — Invitation, not projection
- "Research shows 30-70% of patients..." — Verifiable external claim
- "The exhaustion of over-explaining for the third time" — Universal clinical experience

### ❌ Forbidden (Projection)
- "You are exhausted" — Unearned assumption
- "Your patients don't follow through" — Projecting onto reader
- "You know this is failing" — Dictating their experience

---

## The Two Traps

| Trap | Cause | Result |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Fabrication Trap** | AI invents claims about product or audience | Copy violates Axiom 0-4 (lies) |
| **Airplane Trap** | AI over-corrects to features only | Copy is true but doesn't convert |
| **Easy Button Trap** | AI sells the destination as an instant, effortless fix | Copy lacks credibility with skeptical audiences |

---

## The Honest Destination (The "Beginning of the End")

When selling to skeptical, exhausted professionals, an "instant relief" promise can trigger the Fabrication Trap or simply be dismissed as hype.

**Strategic Nuance:** The destination isn't always the *completion* of the journey; it's often the *beginning of the end* of the pain.

- **False Destination:** "Take this 90-minute course and your burnout is gone." (Fabrication)
- **Airplane:** "90 minutes of coaching theory." (True, but psychologically inert)
- **Honest Destination:** "The beginning of the end of carrying 100% of the responsibility for patient outcomes." (True, psychological, and credible)

**Rule:** Always acknowledge the difficulty of change. Never pretend a "diagnosis" (intro product) is the same as the "cure" (full transformation). Mirror the process, not just the result.

---

## Identity Positioning (Enemy vs. Solution)

When a destination involves an identity shift (e.g., clinician to coach), the "Enemy" must be explicitly named and distinguished from the "Solution" to lower psychological resistance.

- **The Enemy (Identity Threat):** The term that triggers the buyer's ego defense (e.g., "Life Coaching" for clinical OTs).
- **The Solution (Clinical Destination):** The re-framed tool that provides the benefit without the identity risk (e.g., "A Coach Approach").

**Axiom:** Sell the solution as a *skill upgrade*, not an *identity replacement*.

---

## Mirroring the Internal Monologue

Truthful copy mirrors the buyer's **secret self-blame**. 

1. **Identify the Blame**: What does the buyer think is their fault? (e.g., "My patients don't follow through because I'm failing as a clinician").
2. **Offer Absolution**: Re-attribute the failure to a "Missing Piece" of the system or training.
3. **The Hook**: The most powerful destination is the **Cessation of Self-Blame**.

## Practical Application

### Before Axiom 6 (Airplane Trap)
> "Module 1 is a 1.5-hour self-paced course with 13 lessons covering the Coaching Spectrum Framework™. Includes 0.15 AOTA CEUs and a completion certificate."

### After Axiom 6 (Destination)
> "Module 1 is the diagnosis. You'll finally understand why 30-70% of patients nod along and do nothing—and why that's not a reflection of your clinical skill. For $49, you stop wondering 'What did I do wrong?'"

Both are true. Only one sells.

---

## Case Study: Function First Coaching

**Problem:** AI kept fabricating claims about the product. User corrected repeatedly. AI retreated to feature-heavy copy. Live deployment became a spec sheet.

**Solution:** WIP drafts in `work_in_progress/` folder recaptured the destination framing:
- "Absolution & Validation" hook
- "Diagnosis vs. Cure" pivot
- "Map vs. Vehicle" metaphor
- "Emotional tax" urgency (not fake timers)

**Lesson:** The destination was always true. We just needed to stop inventing *new* destinations and start mirroring *real* ones.
