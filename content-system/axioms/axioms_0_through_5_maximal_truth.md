# Maximal Truth Copywriting Standard

This standard defines how to apply direct response triggers without violating the constitutional requirement for **Truth**. It is specifically designed for high-trust audiences (professionals, experts, skeptics).

## 1. The Core Philosophy
Every word must earn its place through truth. For high-trust audiences, **clarity is the conversion mechanism**. Our job is to describe a reality they recognize, not to dictate how they feel or what they experience.

## 2. The Primary Axioms (A0-A4)

These axioms supersede ALL other marketing techniques.

### Axiom 0: Every Word Must Earn Its Place Through Truth
Before applying any persuasion technique, every word must pass the truth test:
1. **Is this verifiably true?** — Can it be proven with evidence?
2. **Is this the reader's actual experience?** — Or am I projecting onto them?
3. **Would this hold up under scrutiny?** — Could a skeptic poke a hole in this?

### Axiom 1: The Three-Part Projection Rule
Projection (claiming to know the reader's experience) is a **privilege, not a right**.
1. **Inarguable Fact First** — Open with something they already know to be true.
2. **Earned Assumption Second** — Only after factual ground is established may you slide in an assumption.
3. **Minimal Per Page** — Use projection sparingly (0-2 per page maximum).
*Note: Projection is forbidden in headlines and opening paragraphs.*

### Axiom 2: Absolutes Are Lies
Banned words: *Always, Never, Nothing, Everything, Everyone, No one.*
Apply qualifiers instead:
- "Most" instead of "all"
- "Often" instead of "always"
- "Many report..." instead of "You will..."

### Axiom 3: Clarity Over Cleverness
The Clarity Test:
1. **What is this?** — Specific name/format.
2. **Who is it for?** — Explicit credential/role.
3. **What will I be able to do after?** — Concrete skill/outcome.
4. **How do I get it?** — Clear path to buy.

### Axiom 4: Mirrors, Not Projectors
Headlines and hooks should be **mirrors** (reflecting reality) not **projectors** (forcing an image).
- **Projector (Violation)**: "You've said everything right. Explained it clearly. Even drew a diagram."
- **The Lie**: Claiming a reader "drew a diagram" is a fabricated detail. Unless you were in the room, it's a projection of a specific action they may not have taken.
- **Mirror (Truth)**: "You've explained it clearly. The patient nodded. And then... they didn't quite follow through. Sound familiar?"
- **The "Earned Projection" Nuance**: Phrases like "You already know" are earned when they anchor a universal internal state (e.g., the suspicion of a training gap) that the reader has already validated by continuing to read.
- **Product-First (Best)**: "Module 1 identifies the 'Expert Trap' where clinicians carry 100% of the responsibility for patient outcomes."

### Axiom 5: Research-to-Impact Methodology
Axioms 0-4 define what NOT to do. Axiom 5 defines HOW to create high-impact truthful headlines:
1. **Research First** — Gather verified claims from reputable sources (NIH, professional associations, peer-reviewed journals)
2. **Product Truth Second** — Document actual product features from delivery materials ("the airplane")
3. **Merge at Intersection** — Create headlines combining research truth + product specificity

### Axiom 7: The Read-Aloud Test (Professional Prose Standard)
Final page copy must be composed of grammatically complete, professional sentences. If a line cannot be read aloud naturally in a conversation between peers, it is not done.
1. **No Fragments**: Avoid the "punchy" fragment style for professional audiences.
2. **Ambiguity Check**: Ensure phrases like "taught to have" are complete.
3. **Peer-to-Peer Tone**: Speak as an expert to an expert.

## 3. Implementation Table: Mirrors vs. Projectors

| Type | Action | Projector (Wrong) | Mirror (Right) | Product-First (Best) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Experience** | Reflecting a struggle. | "You're tired of patient excuses." | "When patients agree but don't follow through..." | "Module 1 provides tools for the 'compliance gap'." |
| **Results** | Describing a dip in stats. | "Your results are slipping." | "When results start to dip..." | "This framework identifies where engagement falls off." |
| **Internal State**| Claiming to know a feeling.| "You know you need this." | "Many clinicians report feeling stuck..." | "Training for the specific clinical roadblock of X." |

## 4. Clinical Evidence Extraction Protocol
Truth must be built on evidence, not marketing templates.
1. **Recursive Source Check**: Search until at least 100 relevant sites/pages are identified.
2. **Anti-Marketing Bias**: Filter out agency/marketing-speak.
3. **Primary Voices Only**: Prioritize clinical forums, professional white papers (AOTA, APTA), and peer-reviewed journals.
4. **VOC Forensic Extraction**: Capture the exact phrases clinicians use to describe their own problems.
5. **Verifiable Citations**: Every major claim in the copy must be backed by verifiable data.

## 5. Global Conversion Standards

Beyond truth, certain operational standards are mandatory for global conversion and risk mitigation.

### 5.1 Currency Formatting Mandate
**MANDATORY**: Always explicitly specify the three-letter ISO currency code (e.g., USD, CAD, EUR) after any currency amount.
- **Reasoning**: A global audience perceives the "$" symbol differently. Ambiguity leads to checkout abandonment, lower trust, and professional chargebacks.
- **Format**: `$XXX [ISO CODE]`
- **Examples**: `$595 USD`, `$49 USD`, `$150 CAD`.
- **Application**: Landing pages, CTAs, upsell scripts, emails, and any price mention.

### 5.2 Trademark Consistency & Sovereignty
Use the appropriate superscript symbols (™ or ®) on the first mention of any product name or framework to maintain legal integrity and brand authority.

**Trademark Meta-Verification Protocol (MANDATORY)**:
Before finalizing any content, the agent must perform an exhaustive automated audit to identify trademark drift. For EACH listed trademark:
1. **Search for Lowercase Variants**: Identify where proper nouns are written in lowercase.
2. **Search for Partial Matches**: Identify where words from a trademarked phrase are missing (e.g., product nickname instead of full legal name).
3. **Search for Missing Symbols**: Find every instance of the phrase missing its required `™` or `®` symbol on first mention.
4. **Search for "The" Prefixing**: Ensure trademarked titles are used as proper nouns without the article "the" (unless contextually required by grammar for a non-standalone mention).
